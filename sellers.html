The roadmap

How selling a home works

  1. Home valuation

    Tania reviews recent comparable sales, your home’s condition, and current competition to recommend a price range. You decide the list price together.

  2. Prepare the home

    Small repairs, decluttering, and a deep clean help buyers see the space at its best. Professional photos come next, since most buyers meet your home online first.

  3. Go to market

    Your home is listed on OneKey MLS and promoted through online marketing and open houses. Showings are scheduled around your routine.

  4. Review offers and negotiate

    Each offer is weighed on price, financing strength, and timing. Tania walks you through the trade-offs and negotiates the terms you choose to pursue.

  5. Contracts and disclosures

    In New York, the seller’s attorney typically prepares the contract of sale. You also complete the state’s property condition disclosure statement, and the buyer’s deposit goes into escrow.

  6. Appraisal, title, and the buyer’s mortgage

    While the buyer’s lender completes its appraisal and review, the title company researches the property. Tania tracks each deadline and keeps you updated.

  7. Closing and move

    The buyer does a final walkthrough, the documents are signed, and the sale proceeds are paid out. You hand over the keys and the sale is complete.

Financial obligations

Costs to plan for

Knowing your likely expenses early helps you estimate what you will take away from the sale.

  • Brokerage commissionAgreed in your listing agreement
  • Attorney feeFor preparing the contract and representing you at closing
  • New York State transfer taxPaid by the seller on most sales
  • New York City transfer taxApplies to sales in Brooklyn and Queens
  • Mortgage payoffThe remaining balance on any existing loan
  • Repairs or creditsAnything agreed with the buyer during negotiation
  • Property tax adjustmentsSettled between buyer and seller at closing
  • Moving costsMovers, storage, and overlap between homes

This page is general information about a typical New York sale. It is not legal, tax, or lending advice. Your attorney and lender will confirm the figures for your transaction.
