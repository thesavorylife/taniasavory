The roadmap

How buying a home works

  1. Start with a conversation

    We talk through what you want, where you want to live, and your timeline. Before touring homes, you will review a buyer representation agreement that explains how Tania works for you.

  2. Get pre-approved

    A lender reviews your income, credit, and savings, then issues a pre-approval letter. That letter shows sellers you are ready and tells you the price range to shop in.

  3. Tour homes

    Tania sets up a search matched to your needs and schedules showings. Seeing homes in person helps you sort the must-haves from the nice-to-haves.

  4. Make an offer

    Once a home feels right, recent comparable sales guide the price and terms. Tania presents your offer and negotiates on your behalf.

  5. Inspection and contracts

    After an offer is accepted, a home inspector checks the property. In New York, attorneys for the buyer and seller prepare and review the contract, and your contract deposit is held in escrow.

  6. Mortgage, appraisal, and title

    Your lender orders an appraisal and completes its review, ending with a mortgage commitment. A title company confirms the seller can transfer clear ownership.

  7. Final walkthrough and closing

    Shortly before closing, you walk through the home to confirm its condition. At the closing table you sign the documents, the funds change hands, and the keys are yours.

Financial obligations

Costs to plan for

The purchase price is only part of the picture. These are the common costs buyers in New York should budget for.

  • Down paymentThe portion of the price you pay up front
  • Contract depositPaid at contract signing and credited toward your purchase
  • Home inspectionPaid to the inspector at the time of the inspection
  • Attorney feeFor contract review and representation at closing
  • Lender fees and appraisalCharged by your mortgage lender
  • Title insuranceProtects against claims on the property’s ownership
  • Mortgage recording taxA New York tax on most new mortgages
  • Mansion taxA New York tax on purchases of $1 million or more
  • Homeowners insuranceRequired by lenders before closing
  • Tax and insurance escrowsPrepaid amounts your lender may collect at closing

This page is general information about a typical New York sale. It is not legal, tax, or lending advice. Your attorney and lender will confirm the figures for your transaction.
